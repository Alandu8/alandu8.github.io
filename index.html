What I'm building toward

Models will soon make most of the forecasts in trading. Whether anyone should trust them is the open problem, and it's the one I'm building around.

  • Forecasts you can audit. Every call tied to point-in-time inputs, a frozen record of what the model saw, and an outcome label written by the market, not by the modeler.
  • Evaluation before capital. Rolling, live, contamination-proof tests that a model cannot have memorized. A backtest is a claim; a live record is evidence.
  • Agents with memory and governance. Idea generation, review, and execution as separate agents behind audited tools and human gates, with memory that compounds instead of resetting every session.
  • Infrastructure as the moat. The point-in-time archive, the outcome labels, and the running system are what compound. The ideas are the easy part.

I've shipped end to end before: a Mandarin blog platform I founded in my first year at HKU that grew past 100k readers, and the multi-agent AI platform I designed from scratch at Capstone, which the whole desk now runs on daily.
